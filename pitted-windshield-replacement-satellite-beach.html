What Does Pitted Windshield Replacement Involve?

Pitted windshield replacement follows the same steps as any windshield job. The old glass is cut out, the frame is cleaned and checked for salt-air rust, fresh urethane is applied and new glass is set and centered. Because the problem is wear rather than a crack, it is a good time to replace worn wiper blades too, so the new surface stays clear longer.

Why Do Windshields Get Pitted Near the Beach?

Pitting comes from thousands of tiny impacts from sand, grit and small road debris hitting the glass at speed. On the barrier island, wind carries sand off the dunes onto A1A and parking lots, and salt spray leaves a gritty film that wipers drag across the surface. Each pit is tiny, but together they scatter light, which is why the glare is worst when the sun is low or headlights are in your eyes.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a pitted windshield a safety issue?

It can be. Heavy pitting does not weaken the glass the way a crack does, but the glare it causes can hide a pedestrian or a car at sunrise or at night. If you find yourself squinting through the windshield, it is time to replace it.

Can a pitted windshield be buffed out?

Light surface haze can sometimes be improved, but real pits go into the glass. Grinding them out thins the glass and can distort your view, so replacement is the safer fix for a heavily pitted windshield.

Does insurance cover a pitted windshield?

It depends on the policy. Some insurers treat pitting as normal wear and tear rather than sudden damage. We can help you check your coverage, and we give a free estimate either way.

How do I slow down pitting on a new windshield?

Rinse salt and sand off with fresh water instead of running the wipers dry, replace wiper blades every six to twelve months and leave more room behind trucks on A1A and the causeways.
