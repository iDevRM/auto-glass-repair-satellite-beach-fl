What Does Back Glass & Door Windows Involve?

A door window job starts with removing the inside door panel and clearing every piece of broken tempered glass from inside the door, the window track and the cabin. The new glass is bolted to the window regulator, adjusted so it runs smoothly and seals at the top, and the panel goes back on. Back glass and liftgate glass are bonded in like a windshield, and the defrost lines and any antenna or wiper connections are hooked up and tested.

Why Does Back Glass Shatter Instead of Crack?

Most back glass and door windows are tempered glass, which is heated and cooled quickly so the surface is under tension. That makes it much stronger than plain glass, but once it breaks the stored stress releases all at once and the whole window crumbles into small cubes. That protects people from sharp shards, but it means rear and side glass cannot be repaired the way a windshield chip can.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can a cracked back window be repaired?

No. Back glass and door windows are usually tempered glass, which cannot be filled with resin like a windshield chip. Once tempered glass is damaged it needs to be replaced, and it often shatters completely within days.

How long does back glass replacement take?

Most door windows take about an hour, including cleanup. Back glass and liftgate glass take a little longer because they are bonded in place and the defrost lines need to be connected and tested.

Is broken back glass covered by insurance in Florida?

Comprehensive coverage usually pays for broken rear and side glass, including break-ins and storm damage. The Florida rule that waives the deductible applies only to windshields, so your regular deductible usually applies here.

What should I do right after a car break-in?

File a police report if anything was taken, then tape heavy plastic over the opening and avoid the seats until the glass is cleaned out. Call us for the replacement and keep the report number for your claim.
